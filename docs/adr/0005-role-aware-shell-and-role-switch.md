# Role-aware shell: User × Role × Role family, active Role out of the URL

Challenge 05 turns a single-persona app (a hardcoded "Dana Reyes, CPA" everywhere) into a role-aware one. Per ADR-0004 this is built **foundation-first**: 05 ships the _architecture that adapts_ so 06/02/03 are correct-by-construction, not the rich content each role sees.

**The model.** Three plain terms. A **User** is an identity (the _who_) that holds one or more **Roles** (_who, as what_). Every Role belongs to a **Role family** (_which side of the firm_) — **Firm** (Preparer, Reviewer, Firm Admin, Seasonal Staff) or **Client** (Individual Taxpayer, Business Owner) — which map onto CONTEXT.md's Preparer/Client actors. The shell renders against the User's **active Role**: pick a Role and its family already tells you the side, so no extra concept is needed. This is what makes the case study's "firm employee who also has a personal Return" expressible: that person is one User holding a Firm Role _and_ a Client Role, switching their active Role to move between them. A single-role User has nothing to switch to, so switching only appears when it means something.

**The active Role is who you are, not where you are — so it stays out of the URL.** Routes remain shared (`/`, `/returns/:id`); the shell reads the active Role from a `RoleProvider` context at the router root and branches content behind the same path. Preparer `/` is the CPA dashboard; Client `/` is a thin status landing. The role switcher replaces the header user badge (no new chrome — the header, layout, and brand stay byte-identical across Roles, which is what keeps it "one product"); the active Role persists to `localStorage` so a reload keeps who you're acting as. Encoding the active Role in the path (`/client/...`) was rejected: it forks the route tree and fights the one-shell goal, and deep links to a Return must keep working — what you _see_ there just depends on your active Role.

**Scope guard.** 05 implements two Roles with real adaptation — **Preparer** (exists) and **Individual Taxpayer / Client** (thin landing) — and models all six Roles as data so the switcher and families are visible, the four unimplemented Roles shown but inert. Two Users are seeded: one single-role (Dana Reyes) and one multi-role (Jordan Avery, a firm staffer who is also the Client on their own Return) to showcase the switch. Permission is communicated at the **shell** level only: nav renders from a per-Role manifest that omits what a Role can't reach; a forbidden route (e.g. a Client deep-linking a Preparer review) renders an explicit role-aware "not available" state with a link home — never a silent 404 or redirect; and the header always names the active Role.

**Consequence:** Field State (challenge 08) is untouched — no per-Field role permissions yet. A Client reaching a Field-bearing surface as read-only is deferred to 01/06, where the existing `locked`-style visual language will be reused so one affordance system holds. Reviewer's intra-firm approval flow is likewise modeled-but-inert until there's more to approve.
